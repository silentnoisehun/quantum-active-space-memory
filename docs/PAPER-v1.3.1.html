Abstract

Passive quantum memory in superconducting architectures—defined as unmonitored residual state retention in idle transmon chips across independent executions—was recently shown to be strictly null across a pooled meta-analysis of 1,016 hardware jobs (Fisher χ² = 24.63, p = 0.135; Stouffer Z = +0.29, p = 0.387; see silentnoisehun/quantum-pooled-holdout). Here we demonstrate that Active Space Memory—where the quantum state is continuously coupled to an electromagnetic cavity field via mid-circuit measurement and fast classical feedforward (Quantum Zeno Dynamics)—dynamically stabilizes the transmon state against coherent rotational perturbations and environmental decoherence.

Executing OpenQASM 3 dynamic circuits on the 156-qubit IBM Quantum Heron processor (ibm_marrakesh), we observe an active Z-basis survival probability of 97.20% ± 1.04 pp (95% CI: [95.98%, 98.06%]) over N = 10 perturbation cycles, compared to 50.10% ± 3.09 pp (95% CI: [47.01%, 53.19%]) in the unmonitored passive control arm. This constitutes a statistically definitive net protection advantage of ΔP|1⟩ = +47.10 pp (95% CI: [43.84 pp, 50.36 pp], two-sample binomial: unpooled z = 28.29, pooled z = 23.91, two-sided p < 10⁻¹⁵).

1 · Physical Model & Paradigm Shift

1.1 · The Limits of Passive Quantum Storage

Classical information storage operates predominantly as a passive container: physical media retain bit values under zero power input. In superconducting transmon architectures, however, an isolated system is strictly open to thermal and radiative environments, undergoing spontaneous relaxation (T₁) and pure dephasing (Tφ). Without continuous active intervention or macroscopic quantum error correction, passive quantum space storage decays monotonically to the thermal ground state.

1.2 · Active Space Coupling via Quantum Zeno Dynamics

By driving the transmon-cavity dispersive interaction Hint = ℏχ(a†a)σz through mid-circuit projective readout and FPGA feedforward (if_else), we construct an active dynamical attractor:

  1. Zeno Projection: Frequent mid-circuit measurements along σz suppress coherent rotations Ωσx with a transition probability scaling quadratically as Pflip ≈ (Ωτ)² ≪ 1.
  2. Conditional Feedforward: If a projection error or decay event (|0⟩) is detected, the FPGA branch logic evaluates with sub-microsecond latency (~300 ns), triggering a fast conditional X pulse (~60 ns). The closed-loop cycle duration is dominated by dispersive resonator readout integration (2,684 ns), yielding an overall stabilization loop time of τloop ≈ 3.0 µs.
  3. Active Medium: The quantum state is dynamically sustained through the continuous energy and information exchange between the transmon and the cavity field.

2 · Experimental Protocol

2.1 · State Preparation and Perturbation

  • Target state: |1⟩ = X|0⟩.
  • Perturbation operator: Rx(θ = 0.15π) per cycle.
  • Active arm: N cycles of [Rx(θ) → Measure(q, cstep) → if cstep == 0: X(q)], followed by terminal measurement.
  • Passive control arm: N unmonitored rotations Rx(θ) without intermediate measurements or feedback, followed by terminal measurement.

2.2 · Metric Definition

We report the Z-basis target survival probability P|1⟩ = N|1⟩ / Ntotal, where N|1⟩ is the count of terminal |1⟩ outcomes over Ntotal = 1,000 physical shots. This metric directly reflects state retention against the applied X-rotation without requiring full quantum state tomography.

Note on readout error ceiling and calibration drift

The nominal backend calibration report at 08:04 UTC indicated an assignment error p(0|1) = 5.47%, which would impose a theoretical static unmitigated detection ceiling of 1 − p(0|1) = 94.53%. The unmitigated physical execution of job dbo63b5t7fqc73akk78g at 11:57 UTC recorded 972/1,000 raw counts (97.20% ± 1.04 pp). This +2.67 pp excess over static nominal calibration reflects empirical temporal drift in transmon discriminator thresholds / readout resonator frequency over the ~4 hours between calibration and execution (indicating real-time p(0|1) ≤ 2.80% during run, inferred from the observed survival under the hypothesis of parameter drift, not directly measured via a contemporaneous isolated |1⟩ calibration circuit), rather than post-hoc error mitigation (all counts reported are unmitigated raw bitstrings).

Academic arbitration on Bell state entanglement

While the active parity circuit triples parity subspace survival (33.60% vs 10.90%, +22.70 pp, unpooled z = 12.69), the state remains 16.40 pp below the bipartite entanglement witness threshold (F > 50.0%). The ZZ parity stabilizer protects computational bit-flip parities, but does not protect against phase dephasing. Genuine bipartite entanglement was not preserved.

3.3 · Physical 3-Qubit GHZ Genuine Multipartite Entanglement (GME) Witness

  • Target backend: ibm_marrakesh (156-qubit Heron r2 processor)
  • Physical hardware job ID: dbo6t18qtr0c73ae383g (submitted 13:08:26 UTC)
  • Topology: 5 physical transmons in a linear chain Q0 — Q1 — Q2 — Q3 — Q4. Logical data qubits q₀, q₁, q₂ map to physical transmons Q0, Q2, Q4; ancilla transmons Q1 and Q3 measure the non-destructive parity stabilizers S₁ = Zq₀Zq₁ and S₂ = Zq₁Zq₂.
  • Target tripartite state: |GHZ₃⟩ = (|000⟩ + |111⟩)/√2
  • Tomographic protocol: 5 Pauli bases (ZZZ, XXX, XYY, YXY, YYX) across 10 circuits
  • Total physical shots: 10,000 (1,000 per basis per arm, execution date 2026-11-08)

Circuit complexity & physical gate overhead

Re-transpiled locally against the live ibm_marrakesh target (Qiskit 2.4.2, optimization_level=1, layouts [0,2,4,1,3] active / [0,2,4] passive):

  • Active arm: 26 native CZ gates, 6 mid-circuit measurements (+3 terminal readout), 6 dynamic resets, 6 conditional feedforward branches. Circuit depth is 59 for the ZZZ basis and 61–62 for the four transverse bases. Total mid-circuit readout integration = 6 × 2,684 ns ≈ 16.1 µs, consuming 46% of transmon dephasing time (T₂ = 35.05 µs).
  • Passive arm: 8 native CZ gates, 0 mid-circuit measurements (3 terminal readout measurements), 0 dynamic resets, 0 feedforward branches. Circuit depth is 19 for the ZZZ basis and 18–23 for the four transverse bases.

Rigorous scientific conclusion — GME falsification & phase decoherence mechanism
  1. Why passive ⟨XXX⟩ = +0.894: the perturbation drive Rx(θ) = exp(−i·θ/2·X) strictly commutes with the Pauli X operator ([Rx(θ), X] = 0). In the passive circuit the drive mathematically cannot rotate the state out of the XXX eigenspace; ⟨XXX⟩ degrades only through background thermal relaxation across its minimal depth (19 gates).
  2. Why active destroys phase coherence (Re[ρ] = −2.63% vs +11.22%): repeated non-destructive ZZ syndrome projections project the superposition onto the Z-eigenbasis subspace, acting as an aggressive phase-dephasing channel that converts (|000⟩ + |111⟩)/√2 into an incoherent classical mixture ½(|000⟩⟨000| + |111⟩⟨111|). Combined with the accumulated noise of 26 physical CZ gates and 16.1 µs of mid-circuit measurement idle dephasing (T₂ = 35.05 µs), phase coherence is completely eradicated.
  3. The arbitration: active Zeno stabilization does NOT preserve genuine multipartite entanglement (Factive = 36.62% ≤ 50.0%). It provides powerful, statistically verified stabilization of classical bit populations (39.25% vs 12.85%) but destroys quantum phase coherence. Preserving tripartite entanglement under continuous Zeno dynamics fundamentally requires transverse stabilizer measurements (e.g. interleaving an XXX stabilizer) to protect the phase degree of freedom.

4 · Limitations and Future Work

  1. Multi-qubit scaling: single-qubit state preservation (Δ = +47.10 pp), 2-qubit entangled Bell-state parity protection (Δ = +22.70 pp) and 3-qubit GHZ population stabilization (>3× advantage over passive decay) have all been demonstrated on physical hardware (ibm_marrakesh). Scaling to 2D topological surface-code tiles and higher-order stabilizer lattices remains the next development milestone.
  2. Metric scope: we measure computational Z-basis retention P|1⟩, Bell-basis parity survival P|Φ+⟩, and five-basis GME witness operators for tripartite GHZ states. Full multi-qubit state tomography and randomized benchmarking across cycle depths (N ∈ [5, 100]) are planned to further quantify phase coherence degradation.
  3. Temporal scope: physical hardware trials evaluated N = 10 single-qubit, N = 4 two-qubit Bell and N = 3 three-qubit GHZ dynamic cycles. Extended testing up to N = 100 cycles will probe the asymptotic equilibrium fidelity limit imposed by two-qubit CZ gate errors and FPGA latency.
  4. Calibration-coupling caveat: the single-qubit arm's survival probability exceeds the static nominal readout-error ceiling (94.53%). We attribute this to temporal calibration drift, and we did not run a contemporaneous isolated |1⟩ calibration circuit in the same job — so the drift hypothesis is an inference from the observed survival, not a direct measurement. A follow-up campaign should pair every Zeno job with a back-to-back calibration circuit to close this gap.

6.2 · Corrections applied in v1.3.1

  1. Active-arm Wilson interval (§3.1 and abstract). v1.3.0 reported [95.96%, 98.08%] for 972/1,000. No standard interval method reproduces that pair under the stated "Wilson" label: Wilson gives [95.983%, 98.056%], Wilson with continuity correction [95.933%, 98.106%], Agresti–Coull 95% [95.967%, 98.071%], Clopper–Pearson [95.979%, 98.132%]. The published value is replaced by the Wilson value — the method named in the table, and the one that reproduces the other three arm intervals of the paper exactly. The abstract half-width changes accordingly from ±1.02 pp to ±1.04 pp.
  2. Bell-arm p-value (§3.2). v1.3.0 quoted a single p = 7.6×10⁻³⁷ next to two z-scores. The exact value for the unpooled statistic is 7.1×10⁻³⁷; the pooled statistic gives 3.0×10⁻³⁴. Both are now stated explicitly, and the unpooled z-score is given as 12.69 (was 12.68) to match the recomputation.
  3. Active-arm CZ count (§3.3). v1.3.0 stated 23 native CZ gates. Re-transpiling the same builder output against the live ibm_marrakesh target yields 26 CZ gates for all five tomographic bases. The circuit-depth figures of v1.3.0 correspond to the ZZZ basis only; the depth ranges across the five bases are now given.
  4. Passive-arm measurement count (§3.3). "0 measurements" is replaced by "0 mid-circuit measurements (3 terminal readout measurements)", which is what the transpiled circuit contains.
  5. Version consistency. The README.md BibTeX block, CITATION.cff and the .zenodo.json description are aligned to v1.3.1, and the Zenodo description quotes the corrected p = 7.1×10⁻³⁷ instead of 3.8×10⁻³⁷.
  6. New limitation (§4.4). The readout-drift hypothesis for the single-qubit arm is explicitly flagged as an inference, not a contemporaneous measurement.
